Predictive Analytics

Six classification and regression projects across healthcare, HR, real estate, and finance. The common thread: picking the model that fits the decision, not just the one with the best headline number — cost-optimized thresholds for attrition, interpretability over black-box gains for hospital stays, different models for different forecast horizons.

Heart Disease Prediction

Binary classification of heart disease using CDC BRFSS survey data (300k+ respondents, 18 predictors). Compared additive logistic regression, a GLMNET-selected non-additive model with polynomial and interaction terms, LDA, QDA, and kNN. The non-additive logistic model performed best (AUROC 0.845), substantially outperforming LDA (0.637), QDA (0.622), and kNN (0.586). General health was the strongest signal: poor self-rated health carried 6.57 times the odds of heart disease relative to excellent (95% CI: 6.01–7.18). Class imbalance held sensitivity low across all models, a limitation I discuss rather than tune away.

Time Series Forecasting — Eli Lilly (LLY)

Forecast LLY monthly log price (136 observations, 2015–2026) for a healthcare investment fund needing both quarterly and annual horizons. Compared ARMA, ARIMA(4,1,0), two VAR specifications, and an MLP neural network. No single model won both horizons: ARIMA was best at three months (ASE 0.004) but degraded badly at twelve (ASE 0.355), while the MLP with lagged XLV was best long-term (ASE 0.017). Notably, VAR with Novo Nordisk outperformed VAR with the sector ETF — the direct GLP-1 competitor carried more signal than the sector as a whole.

Hospital Length of Stay Prediction

Regression modeling of average patient length of stay across 113 hospitals. Compared an interpretable additive MLR against a complex MLR with polynomial and interaction terms (LASSO-guided) and a kNN model. The complex MLR performed best on every error metric (R² = 0.776, RMSE = 0.0996, MAE = 0.077); kNN was worst, trading interpretability for nothing. Infection risk was significant across all specifications: each one-unit increase was associated with a 5.4% increase in median length of stay (bootstrap 95% CI: 3.3–7.6%).

Employee Attrition Prediction — Frito-Lay

Classification modeling to identify turnover risk in Frito-Lay HR data, with the decision threshold tuned to minimize expected cost rather than maximize accuracy. Age, job role, and overtime emerged as the dominant predictors. The kNN model (k = 3, threshold 0.15) reached 94.29% sensitivity at 72.76% accuracy — deliberately favoring recall, since missing an employee who leaves costs 50–400% of their salary to replace versus $200 for a retention incentive. Translated model output into a projected cost range of $379k–$2.53M, down from $1.27M–$9.59M under the Naive Bayes alternative.

Sale Price Prediction

Predicted residential sale prices in Ames, Iowa (1,460 homes, 79 variables) for two distinct questions: a neighborhood-level inference problem for a realtor client, and a city-wide prediction problem scored on Kaggle. For the first, log-transformed both price and living area to satisfy regression assumptions, then tested whether the price-per-square-foot relationship varies by neighborhood — it does (F = 8.649, p = 0.0002), with BrkSide showing a significantly steeper slope than NAmes or Edwards. For the second, compared four specifications; the final MLR incorporating quality indices, building type, age, and neighborhood explained 86.7% of variance in log sale price and achieved a Kaggle RMSLE of 0.150, improving on the simple-regression baseline of 0.229. Validated assumptions throughout with Q-Q, leverage, Cook's D, and Durbin-Watson diagnostics.

Crab Age Prediction

Regression competition predicting crab age from physical measurements, with an interactive R Shiny app for exploring predictions. Feature engineering — polynomial terms, a constructed shell-density variable, and size-by-sex interactions — produced a final model with R² = 0.702 and MAE = 1.14 months.

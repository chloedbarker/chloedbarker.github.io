Predictive Analytics

Six classification and regression projects across healthcare, HR, real estate, and finance. The common thread is picking the model that fits the decision, not just the one with the best headline number, using cost-optimized thresholds for attrition, interpretability over black-box gains for hospital stays, and different models for different forecast horizons.

Heart Disease Prediction

Used CDC survey data on 300K+ people to predict who's at risk for heart disease. I compared five different models, and a more flexible logistic regression came out on top, with self-reported general health turning out to be one of the strongest predictors, even more than several clinical factors.

R RStudio Logistic Regression GLMNET KNN LDA / QDA ROC / AUC

Time Series Forecasting — Eli Lilly (LLY)

Forecasted Eli Lilly's stock price for a healthcare investment fund that needed both short and long-term outlooks. I tried several time-series models and found no single winner, one was best for three-month forecasts and a different one was best for a full year, which was a good lesson in matching the model to the actual question being asked.

R RStudio tswge ARIMA VAR MLP Neural Net Time Series

Hospital Length of Stay Prediction

Predicted how long patients would stay in the hospital using data from 113 hospitals. I compared a simple, easy-to-explain model against a more complex one, and the complex model won on accuracy, but I made sure it still made sense before trusting it over the simpler one.

R RStudio Linear Regression LASSO KNN Bootstrap CI

Employee Attrition Prediction — Frito-Lay

Built a model to flag Frito-Lay employees at risk of leaving, then tuned it around the real cost of turnover instead of just chasing accuracy, since missing someone who's about to quit is a lot more expensive than a false alarm. Age, job role, and overtime were the biggest predictors.

Sale Price Prediction

Predicted home sale prices in Ames, Iowa two ways: first digging into whether the price-per-square-foot relationship changes by neighborhood (it does), then building a model that competed on Kaggle and explained about 87% of the variation in price.

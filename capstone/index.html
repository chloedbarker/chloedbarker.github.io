Groundwater Lithium & Dementia Prevalence in Texas

Co-authored with Tracy Dower  ·  Advised by Jacob Turner, PhD & Jacquelyn Cheun, PhD

Python Pandas / GeoPandas OLS Regression HC3 Robust SEs Permutation Testing Bootstrap CIs Geospatial Analytics SQL / MySQL

Problem

Lithium occurs naturally in groundwater at concentrations that vary widely across Texas counties. A growing body of epidemiological literature suggests low-dose lithium exposure may be associated with reduced risk of neurodegenerative disease, but evidence at the population level — and in Texas specifically — remains limited.

Research question: Do Texas counties with higher, more consistent groundwater lithium concentrations exhibit lower dementia prevalence, after accounting for small-sample constraints and exposure heterogeneity?

This project tests that question using observational county-level data and robust statistical methods appropriate for a small-sample ecological study.

Data

  • Groundwater quality: Texas Water Development Board (TWDB) well measurements compiled into a county-level dataset with all water quality parameters.
  • Health outcome: NORC Dementia DataHub county-level dementia prevalence estimates, 2020.
  • Geography: Texas county FIPS crosswalk and Census TIGER shapefiles for choropleth mapping.

A key engineering challenge was ensuring consistent county identifiers (FIPS codes) across all three sources and resolving naming mismatches prior to merging.

Fig. 2. Side-by-side choropleth maps of Texas counties displaying mean groundwater lithium concentration (μg/L, pre-2020; TWDB, 2024) and 2020 dementia prevalence among Medicare beneficiaries under age 75 (NORC, 2024). Gray counties indicate missing lithium data. Higher lithium concentrations are concentrated in the Panhandle and Trans-Pecos regions.

Approach

  • Exposure homogeneity classification: counties classified using a three-criterion framework — coefficient of variation ≤ 0.50, ≥ 70% well consistency, and ≥ 10 samples — to ensure only counties with stable, reliable lithium readings were included in modeling.
  • Ingestion & cleaning: standardized dates, resolved FIPS mismatches, handled missingness, and validated county naming across all sources.
  • Feature engineering: aggregated well-level measurements to county-level summaries; constructed map-ready tables for choropleth visualization.
  • Exploration: choropleth maps and summary statistics characterizing spatial distribution of lithium concentrations and dementia prevalence across Texas.
  • Modeling: OLS regression with HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors on the homogeneous county subset (N ≈ 27–30), supplemented with permutation testing and bootstrap confidence intervals.
  • Sensitivity analyses: varied classification thresholds (CV cutoff, minimum samples, well consistency floor) to assess robustness of findings.

Results

  • Identified a subset of Texas counties with sufficiently homogeneous groundwater lithium exposure for reliable inference; final analytic sample ranged from 27–30 counties depending on classification parameters.
  • OLS regression with HC3 robust standard errors found a negative association between groundwater lithium concentration and county-level dementia prevalence — consistent with the direction reported in prior epidemiological literature.
  • Permutation tests and bootstrap confidence intervals supported the robustness of the primary finding under small-sample constraints.
  • Sensitivity analyses across classification threshold variants yielded directionally consistent results, strengthening confidence in the primary specification.
  • Produced choropleth maps illustrating spatial variation of lithium concentrations and dementia prevalence across Texas counties.
⚠️ Important caveat: This is an ecological, observational study. Results reflect county-level correlations and should not be interpreted as causal. Confounding, ecological fallacy, and data limitations are discussed in the full paper.

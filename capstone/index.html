Groundwater Lithium & Dementia Prevalence in Texas

Co-authored with Tracy Dower  ·  Advised by Jacob Turner, PhD & Jacquelyn Cheun, PhD

Python Jupyter SQL · MySQL pandas · GeoPandas OLS · HC3 Robust SEs Permutation Testing Bootstrap CIs Geospatial Analysis

Problem

Lithium occurs naturally in groundwater at concentrations that vary widely across Texas counties. A growing body of epidemiological literature suggests low-dose lithium exposure may be associated with reduced risk of neurodegenerative disease, but evidence at the population level, and in Texas specifically, remains limited.

Research question: Do Texas counties with higher, more consistent groundwater lithium concentrations exhibit lower dementia prevalence, after accounting for small-sample constraints and exposure heterogeneity?

This project tests that question using observational county-level data and robust statistical methods appropriate for a small-sample ecological study.

Data

  • Groundwater quality: 460,137 Texas Water Development Board (TWDB) well measurements (1919–2019) compiled into a county-level dataset with all water quality parameters.
  • Health outcome: NORC Dementia DataHub county-level dementia prevalence estimates, 2020.
  • Geography: Texas county FIPS crosswalk and Census TIGER shapefiles for choropleth mapping.

A key engineering challenge was ensuring consistent county identifiers (FIPS codes) across all three sources and resolving naming mismatches prior to merging.

Fig. 2. Side-by-side choropleth maps of Texas counties displaying mean groundwater lithium concentration (μg/L, pre-2020; TWDB, 2024) and 2020 dementia prevalence among Medicare beneficiaries under age 75 (NORC, 2024). Gray counties indicate missing lithium data. Higher lithium concentrations are concentrated in the Panhandle and Trans-Pecos regions.

Approach

  • Exposure homogeneity classification: counties classified using a three-criterion framework, at least 10 samples, at least 70% of wells in one tail of the statewide distribution, and a coefficient of variation ≤ 0.50, to identify counties where a county mean is a defensible proxy for population exposure.
  • Ingestion & cleaning: standardized dates, resolved FIPS mismatches, handled missingness, and validated county naming across all sources.
  • Feature engineering: aggregated well-level measurements to county-level summaries; constructed map-ready tables for choropleth visualization.
  • Exploration: choropleth maps and summary statistics characterizing spatial distribution of lithium concentrations and dementia prevalence across Texas.
  • Modeling: OLS regression with HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors on the homogeneous county subset (N = 14), supplemented with permutation testing, bootstrap confidence intervals, and Cook's D / leave-one-out diagnostics.
  • Sensitivity analyses: varied classification thresholds (CV cutoff, minimum samples, well consistency floor) to assess robustness of findings; also tested model sensitivity to sodium as a confounding covariate.

Results

Across the 240 counties with complete data, groundwater lithium showed effectively no association with dementia prevalence (r = −0.095, R² = 0.009). Rather than treat this as the end of the analysis, I examined why the null appeared. A county-mean lithium value averages residents drawing from wells with substantially different concentrations, and this measurement error in the predictor attenuates regression coefficients toward zero regardless of the underlying effect.

  • Developed a three-criterion exposure homogeneity framework, at least 10 samples, at least 70% of wells in one tail of the statewide distribution, and a coefficient of variation of 0.50 or below, to identify the 14 counties in which a county mean is a defensible proxy for population exposure.
  • Within that subsample, explanatory power increased from R² = 0.009 to R² = 0.226. The lithium coefficient was negative, and 86% of leave-one-out slopes and 66% of bootstrap slopes remained in the protective direction, indicating the result was not driven by any single county.
  • Found that sodium confounding alone is sufficient to reverse the sign of the association, without adjusting for sodium, the lithium coefficient becomes positive, so controlling for geochemical co-variates is essential rather than optional in this analysis.
  • Produced choropleth maps showing lithium concentrated in the Panhandle and Trans-Pecos regions, which also show lower dementia prevalence.
Limitations: At N = 14, the primary model's p-value is 0.73 and the bootstrap confidence interval spans zero. These results are directionally consistent with the existing literature but are not statistically significant, and the ecological design does not support causal inference. The methodological contribution is the homogeneity framework itself, which extends to other trace elements and health outcomes studied at the county level.
